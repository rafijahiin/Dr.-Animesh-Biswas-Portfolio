The first night in Cox's Bazar, I could not sleep. Not from the heat, not from the briefings, not from anything I could name in a report. It was the smell of damp tarpaulin in the air, and the sound of generators running half a kilometre away, and the way the moon looked the same as it did in my own neighbourhood in Dhaka, but somehow not.

I had brought a small notebook. I wrote one line that night:

> *People are not numbers, but tonight everyone is counting.*

It is many years later now, and I have learned that the difference between a death surveillance system and a memorial is mostly a matter of follow-through.
